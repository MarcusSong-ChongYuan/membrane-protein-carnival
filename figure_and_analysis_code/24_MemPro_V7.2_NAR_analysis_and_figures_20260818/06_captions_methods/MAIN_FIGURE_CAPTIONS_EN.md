# Main-figure captions

## Figure 1 | From heterogeneous records to a traceable membrane-target resource

**a,** Release workflow separating source ingestion, canonical identity and entity granularity, deduplication and evidence lineage, and frozen release artifacts. **b,** Flow from contributing databases through evidence modalities to BE tiers; ribbon width is the number of public evidence records, not the number of independent experiments. **c,** Fraction of 7,800 formal proteins covered by mapped expression, subcellular localization, a formal compound interaction, a disease relation or a binding-site assertion. **d,** Principal entity and record denominators. Counts are taken from MemPro V7.2 and are not additive across entity types.

## Figure 2 | A five-axis framework reveals structured membrane-protein organization

**a,** Mosaic of membrane class A/B/C and membrane-evidence level E1–E3; width encodes class abundance and height encodes evidence composition. **b,** Standardized residuals for primary membrane role versus primary molecular function. Red and blue cells indicate over- and under-representation relative to independence, respectively (Pearson χ²; bias-corrected Cramer's V=0.619; cell-wise statistics and BH-FDR are supplied). **c,** Primary-category flow across membrane class, membrane role, molecular function and biological process. Complete detailed annotations remain in source data. **d,** Distribution of normal-tissue RNA specificity Tau by membrane role for groups with n≥30 proteins (Kruskal–Wallis P=4.34×10^-92). Boxes show median and interquartile range; violins show the distribution. **e,** Informative-annotation coverage across the five axes. Placeholder and unresolved states are shown rather than omitted.

## Figure 3 | Chemical diversity is broad, whereas target-family preference is modest

**a,** Rank–abundance distribution of Bemis–Murcko scaffolds among 240,055 interaction-linked compounds; representative common non-acyclic scaffolds are shown. **b,** Robust within-property z-score distributions for molecular weight, XlogP, TPSA and rotatable-bond count across biological-status strata. These are descriptive properties, not drug-likeness decisions. **c,** Compound target breadth versus normalized entropy across membrane roles. High entropy indicates cross-role polypharmacology. **d,** Structure-class × membrane-role enrichment, with colour representing log2 odds ratio and size representing pair count. The overall association is weak (Cramer's V=0.046). **e,** Morgan/Tanimoto chemical similarity versus target-set Jaccard overlap in a deterministic pair sample; colour represents observations per hexagonal bin. **f,** Separation of the 646,670-compound registry from the 240,055 interaction-linked subset and 406,615 compounds without a formal pair. UMAP is provided only as Supplementary Figure S1.

## Figure 4 | Database coverage and evidence independence are distinct dimensions

**a,** UpSet representation of pair intersections among six major sources. **b,** Pair-level Jaccard similarity between contributing databases. **c,** Distinct contributing database count versus putative independent experiment-lineage count. The latter is inferred from lineage keys and is not a manually audited publication-experiment count. **d,** Lorenz curve of evidence-record concentration across formal pairs (Gini=0.361). **e,** ECDF of mapped/requested source-residue fractions by site tier. **f,** Membrane-side resolution for all 55,610 site assertions; 48,331 unknown-side records remain explicit.

## Figure 5 | Ontology-derived disease and anatomical context

**a,** Multi-label disease anatomy projected to representative anatomical-system positions; bubble area is proportional to fractional disease count so that diseases mapped to multiple systems do not receive full weight in every system. **b,** Standardized residuals for therapeutic area × anatomy among significant cells with adequate counts. **c,** UpSet representation of disease-evidence-channel combinations. **d,** Evidence-level composition of 6,753 formal protein–disease relations: very high, 1,411; high, 3,196; medium, 2,146. Anatomy is mapped for 2,926 of 3,739 formal diseases; 813 remain unmapped. **e,** Bounded disease network showing the top 120 edges with at least two shared targets and Jaccard ≥0.20; only selected high-degree nodes are labelled.

## Figure 6 | Integrated evidence exposes chemically underexplored disease-relevant targets

**a,** Transparent target landscape: x-axis, formal compound-pair coverage; y-axis, disease-support index (3×very high + 2×high + medium); point size, normal-tissue anatomical concordance; colour, coordinate-ready site count. Open circles indicate the hypothesis-generation subset. **b,** Explicit encoding key. **c,** Degree-preserving permutation background for disease–expression anatomical concordance. Observed mean=0.4866; null mean=0.3273; z=44.18; empirical P=0.004975 (200 permutations). **d,** Leading candidates under the bounded transparent rule; each contributing dimension remains visible. **e,** Recommended follow-up sequence. These analyses generate hypotheses and do not establish disease causality or validate binding.
